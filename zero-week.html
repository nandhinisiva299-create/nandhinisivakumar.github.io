Every beginning carries a certain uncertainty. You walk into a room not knowing who you will meet, what you will create, or how much the experience will change you.

For us, PRICE ProtoSem began exactly there.

It was not simply the beginning of a programme. It was the beginning of a shift—from learning concepts to experiencing them, from working individually to thinking collectively, and from having answers to learning how to ask better questions.

DAY 01

ENTER: A Room Full of Strangers

The first day began with unfamiliar faces, awkward glances and quiet tables. But unfamiliarity did not last long.

Introductions became conversations. Conversations revealed strengths. And strengths slowly became the foundation for collaboration. Before we knew it, the room had moved from a collection of individuals to a space filled with possibilities.

"Sometimes, the best ideas begin with people you have never met before."
DAY 02

DISCOVER: Understanding Ourselves Before Building

Before we could understand the people around us, we had to understand ourselves.

Through the 16Personalities assessment, we explored our working styles, strengths and blind spots. The experience became more meaningful when we connected those traits to stories that reflected our individual motivations.

The self-pitch pushed that reflection one step further. Standing up and presenting ourselves was not merely an introduction—it was an exercise in understanding who we are, what we bring to the table, and how we choose to be seen.

"Knowing yourself is the first prototype you ever build."
DAY 03

BUILD: When Chaos Became Collaboration

Then came spaghetti, marshmallows, thread and tape. It sounded simple. It wasn't.

The challenge was never really about constructing the tallest or most perfect tower. It was about experimenting under pressure, communicating quickly, adapting when things failed and understanding how a team behaves when time is limited.

Structures collapsed. Ideas competed. And somewhere between the chaos and the laughter, we discovered something important:

"A team isn't created simply by putting people together. It is built through interaction, friction, trust and shared problem-solving."
DAY 04

CREATE: From Knowing to Making

The next shift was from consuming knowledge to creating with it.

Our exploration of AI and prompting introduced a different way of thinking. Instead of treating technology as a magic answer machine, we began to see it as a collaborative partner—one that responds to the quality of our questions, instructions and iterations.

"Technology becomes powerful when human curiosity knows how to direct it."
DAY 05

INSPIRE: Looking Beyond the Classroom

By Day 5, the lens widened. The PRICE Inauguration marked an important transition: from student activities towards industry-oriented thinking. The central idea was that retail is not simply about selling products—it is about designing meaningful, comprehensive human experiences.

We heard real journeys of founders who started somewhere and kept moving forward—from Ramakrishna Muthu of Thulasi Pharmacy to Swathi of A Toddler Thing.

"Every founder started somewhere. Entrepreneurship is a continuous journey of learning, experimenting and moving forward."

The Beginning, Not The End

Looking back, Zero Week was never about having everything figured out. It was about stepping into uncertainty, meeting collaborators, and building the mindset to turn questions into prototypes.

"The first week wasn't about having all the answers. It was about learning how to ask better questions."
