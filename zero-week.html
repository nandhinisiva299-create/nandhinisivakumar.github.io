Every beginning carries a certain uncertainty. You walk into a room not knowing who you will meet, what you will create, or how much the experience will change you.

For us, PRICE ProtoSem began exactly there.

It was not simply the beginning of a programme. It was the beginning of a shift—from learning concepts to experiencing them, from working individually to thinking collectively, and from having answers to learning how to ask better questions.

01

ENTER

A Room Full of Strangers

The first day began with unfamiliar faces, awkward glances and quiet tables.

But unfamiliarity did not last long.

Introductions became conversations. Conversations revealed strengths. And strengths slowly became the foundation for collaboration. Before we knew it, the room had moved from a collection of individuals to a space filled with possibilities.

The first lesson was subtle but powerful:

Sometimes, the best ideas begin with people you have never met before.
02

DISCOVER

Understanding Ourselves Before Building Together

Before we could understand the people around us, we had to understand ourselves.

Through the 16Personalities assessment, we explored our working styles, strengths and blind spots. The experience became more meaningful when we connected those traits to stories that reflected our individual motivations.

The self-pitch pushed that reflection one step further.

Standing up and presenting ourselves was not merely an introduction—it was an exercise in understanding who we are, what we bring to the table, and how we choose to be seen.

The LinkedIn onboarding session extended that idea into the professional world, reminding us that personal development and professional identity are deeply connected.

Knowing yourself is the first prototype you ever build.
03

BUILD

When Chaos Became Collaboration

Then came spaghetti, marshmallows, thread and tape.

It sounded simple.

It wasn't.

The challenge was never really about constructing the tallest or most perfect tower. It was about experimenting under pressure, communicating quickly, adapting when things failed and understanding how a team behaves when time is limited.

Plans changed. Structures collapsed. Ideas competed.

And somewhere between the chaos and the laughter, we discovered something important:

A team isn't created simply by putting people together. It is built through interaction, friction, trust and shared problem-solving.

The tower may have been temporary.

The lesson wasn't.

04

CREATE

From Knowing to Making

The next shift was from consuming knowledge to creating with it.

Our exploration of AI and prompting introduced a different way of thinking. Instead of treating technology as a magic answer machine, we began to see it as a collaborative partner—one that responds to the quality of our questions, instructions and iterations.

It was an introduction to the input → output → refinement mindset.

The takeaway was simple:

Technology becomes powerful when human curiosity knows how to direct it.
05

INSPIRE

Looking Beyond the Classroom

By Day 5, the lens widened.

The PRICE Inauguration marked an important transition: from student activities towards industry-oriented thinking. The central idea was that retail is not simply about selling products—it is about designing meaningful, comprehensive human experiences. The week also introduced the PAFE framework as a way of elevating our perspective towards real-world industry standards.

The entrepreneurial sessions carried that perspective further.

We heard real journeys of people who started somewhere, kept learning and continued moving forward. From Ramakrishna Muthu of Thulasi Pharmacy, whose journey moved from Medical Representative to Entrepreneur to Managing Director, to Swathi of A Toddler Thing, whose story demonstrated the power of action and execution, the message was clear: entrepreneurship rarely begins with a perfect plan.

Every founder started somewhere.

The Five-Day Shift

ENTER

We stepped into a new world as strangers.

DISCOVER

We understood ourselves and learned to articulate our strengths.

BUILD

We learned that teams are shaped through interaction and experimentation.

CREATE

We discovered how technology can amplify the way we think and execute.

INSPIRE

We looked beyond ourselves and into the wider entrepreneurial ecosystem.

But perhaps the most valuable changes were the ones that could not be measured.

We learned that different perspectives are not obstacles—they are useful.

We learned that ideas do not have to work perfectly the first time. They need to be built, tested and adapted.

We learned that a functional team is created through friction, communication and shared experiences.

We learned that technology can fundamentally change how we approach problems.

And we learned that entrepreneurship is not simply a designation or career path.

It is a continuous journey of learning, experimenting and moving forward.

The Beginning, Not The End

Looking back, Zero Week was never about having everything figured out. It was about stepping into uncertainty and being willing to explore it. It was about meeting strangers who could become collaborators. It was about discovering ourselves before attempting to build something for others.

"The first week wasn't about having all the answers. It was about learning how to ask better questions."

The prototype has only just begun. And perhaps, so have we.
